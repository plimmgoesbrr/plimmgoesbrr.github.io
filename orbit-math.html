// orbit · math & physics

The universe's source code

Physics asks how the universe behaves. Maths, for reasons nobody fully understands, turns out to be the language it answers in. Equations scribbled on paper predict where planets will be in a thousand years. That should be impossible, and it isn't.

// the mystery

Unreasonably effective

In 1960 the physicist Eugene Wigner wrote an essay called The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Mathematics in the Natural Sciences. His point: mathematicians often invent ideas purely because they're beautiful, and decades later physicists discover that the universe runs on exactly those ideas.

  • Imaginary numbers started as a "useless" trick for solving equations. Now quantum mechanics can't be written without them.
  • Curved geometry was a 19th-century curiosity. Then Einstein needed it to describe gravity as bent spacetime.
  • Maxwell's four equations describe electricity and magnetism. Solve them, and out pops a wave moving at exactly the speed of light. Light, it turned out, is electricity and magnetism.

// the most beautiful equation

eiπ + 1 = 0

Euler's identity links five of the most important numbers in maths, each from a completely different corner:

numberwhere it comes from
0nothing; the starting point of counting
1one; the building block of every other number
π ≈ 3.14159circles: how many diameters fit around the edge
e ≈ 2.71828growth: compound interest, populations, radioactive decay
i = √−1the imaginary number nobody believed in at first

The simple version: multiplying by i means "turn 90°". Raising e to an imaginary power means "walk around a circle". So eiπ means "walk halfway around a circle of radius 1", which lands you exactly at −1. Add 1, and you get 0.

Circles, growth and rotation, all secretly the same idea. That's what I mean by poetry.
